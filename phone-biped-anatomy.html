A short, wide starting point

Place the phone directly above the hips. Carry the battery in the pelvis to keep mass low. Use a dedicated controller for balance, with the app providing intentions such as “face me” and “come closer.”

Motion architecture

Look first. Then move.

1 / Find and face a person

The app detects a consenting person through the unobstructed phone camera. It sends a target bearing. The neck pans first; the feet remain planted. Limited neck travel avoids cable winding.

2 / Turn the body

When more rotation is needed, plan small alternating steps using hip yaw. Do not twist both rubber soles against the floor.

3 / Walk closer

Transfer weight → lift one foot → swing → confirm contact → transfer again. Range and edge sensors constrain the path. Lose the target or connection: enter a locally controlled safe stop.

Hardware + app boundary

Two brains, clear jobs.

Phone app · perception and interaction

Camera-based person bearing, voice, screen face, pairing, movement permissions and a stop control. Bluetooth carries high-level commands; optional Wi-Fi handles heavier data.

Robot controller · balance and protection

Read IMU, joint encoders and foot loads locally. Run gait control and actuator limits independently of phone or cloud latency. A hardware stop, watchdog and battery protection live on the robot.

Proposed joints · 14 actuated axes

Each leg: hip yaw, hip roll, hip pitch, knee pitch, ankle pitch and ankle roll (12 total). Phone neck: pan and tilt (2). The first drawing keeps the neck tilt neutral.

Before motor selection

Measure every mass and joint lever arm. Check continuous torque, peak stepping loads, gearbox backlash, foot clearance and thermal limits. Use a tether and dummy phone for initial standing and step tests; include a padded cradle and retaining strap.

Concept study, not a proven walking mechanism. Start on a level indoor floor with short steps; stairs and rough terrain need additional development. Figure built with Anatomy. Balance background: MIT Underactuated Robotics.
