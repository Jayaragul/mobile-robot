How to interpret a faster robot

Speed is only one measurement.

Compare designs on the same surface and phone mass. A lower period speeds the gait cycle; a higher amplitude increases joint motion. Watch tilt, foot contact, slip and effort together.

Mean speed is forward displacement divided by simulated time after the one-second setup, including the startup ramp. Contact slip is summed over both feet and is not the robot’s travel distance. A fast result may still be a shuffle.

The screen is a view of computed physics geometry. No target path or hidden support moves the robot. Pushes apply a horizontal 4 N force to the pelvis for 160 ms; they do not drag the model directly.

Model assumptions

A sandbox, not hardware certification.

  • 14 axes, ideal body-angle feedback, and an assumed linear joint torque–speed envelope: available torque falls to zero at the selected no-load speed.
  • V3 retains the landscape phone, uses 80 mm links and 110 × 68 mm soles, with lower foot mass targets. These need structural and actuator validation.
  • Robot–floor contact is enabled. Robot self-collision, sensor delay/noise, battery draw, gearbox backlash and temperature are not modelled.
  • The controller remains experimental. Ramps and obstacles are challenge scenes; success is not assumed.
